Analytical Insights

Key take‑aways behind the prioritization framework
Binding and docking are complementary, not redundant
Across the curated set, binding and docking scores show near‑zero correlation (Pearson r ≈ −0.06). A compound strong on one metric isn't reliably strong on the other — both are needed for a defensible score.
CLK1 dominates the interaction landscape
CLK1 alone accounts for 71 compounds and 12 of the 41 high‑affinity hits — the single largest node in the network, followed by DYRK1A (61) and HIPK2 (57).
Strong ≠ selective
Of the 41 high‑affinity hits, 28 (68%) show SI worst < 1, indicating genuine target preference — the remaining 13 bind broadly and warrant off‑target scrutiny before further prioritization.
Note: figures reflect an analytical prioritization framework (binding, docking, and selectivity‑index thresholds). Rankings are screening‑stage evidence, not proof of therapeutic efficacy or experimental validation.
